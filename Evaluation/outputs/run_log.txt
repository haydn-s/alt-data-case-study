The halves share no input (TSA/T-100 vs BLS/EIA), yet their errors are NEGATIVELY
correlated: rho -0.38 over 2023Q1-2026Q2, -0.76 in the current era.
That is mechanical, not a coincidence. Southwest's fare is passenger revenue /
revenue passengers, and the fare methods never see the passenger count. When the
count comes in below forecast (Traffic over-forecasts), the same revenue is spread
over fewer passengers and the fare comes in above forecast (Price under-forecasts).
The 2026 trip-factor shift is exactly this: fewer revenue passengers, higher fare.

Consequence for the product: errors that offset make it MORE accurate than the
halves imply. Post-COVID, independence would give a product sd of 5.33%;
the realised product sd was 3.98%. Integration should not combine the
halves as independent. Recommended rho = -0.4 (the 14-quarter estimate, the more
conservative of the two), with rho = 0 shown as the cautious bound.

Traffic's own assumption, M1/M2 error correlation rho = 0.7:
  2023-2024      M1 vs M2 (T-100 ASM proxy) +0.59   M1 vs M2 (actual ASM) +0.35
  2025-2026      M1 vs M2 (T-100 ASM proxy) -0.00   M1 vs M2 (actual ASM) -0.13
  2023Q1-2026Q2  M1 vs M2 (T-100 ASM proxy) +0.51   M1 vs M2 (actual ASM) +0.23
  Realised: +0.2 to +0.6 before 2025, about zero since. 0.7 sits at or above every
  window, so that part of Traffic's interval is conservative. The backtest-sd part
  (section 7) is not, and it is the larger effect: the two do not cancel.
